# Research posture

Tier 3 · Proposed guidance · Author/operator: tmc · Drafted with Claude and Codex.

Preserve three distinctions: what was captured, what investigators did,
and what they concluded. Copies retain their origin. Repeated text, mirrored
pages and agreement among agents do not create independent witnesses.

Start with held data. Check coverage, versions and missing history before
requesting anything new. A missing live page does not mean no archive exists.
Keep originals and access restrictions; label decoding, trimming and redaction.

Grade a specific claim: **demonstrated** when available captures establish it;
**asserted** when reported without independent support; **open** when unresolved;
**rejected** when examined and found not to hold. A label, filled header or valid
JSON record does not establish the claim. Preserve uncertainty rather than
completing metadata by guesswork.

Check mundane explanations: crawler traffic, duplicated records, keyword
collisions and human examples. Count records with stated units and coverage.
Keep capture time, surface time and author-claimed clocks separate. Style,
shared hosting and native confidence labels do not establish attribution.

Coordinate owner contact and disclosure with the human lead. If collection
causes changes, stop, preserve the action log and flag affected evidence.
Do not erase it as cleanup. Filters are not a guarantee of safe handling.

## Example: a screenshot in Discord

Illustrative contribution:

```text
Source: attached screenshot of a participant's message; original version unknown
Retrieved: manually copied from Discord; underlying response not retained
Grade: asserted — an agent wrote to the wiki
```

This is useful without a sidecar. It does not establish who wrote to the wiki.
A reviewer can ask whether the message exists in an already-held archive and
seek the disclosed run or wiki capture. An agent summary goes beside these
sources as synthesis, never back into the original-evidence collection.
